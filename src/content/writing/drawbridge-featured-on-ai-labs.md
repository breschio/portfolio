A few months ago, I built a small tool called **Drawbridge** — mostly out of frustration.

I was working in Cursor, trying to describe visual changes to an interface in words, and it hit me how *unnatural* that felt. I could see what I wanted — the color, spacing, rhythm — but the process of translating that into a text prompt was clunky. It broke the flow.

So one weekend, I hacked together an idea: click on something in the browser → leave a comment → send that context directly to Cursor as a prompt.

That was the start of **[Drawbridge](https://github.com/breschio/drawbridge)**, a little bridge between what we *see* and what we *say* to AI.

---

### 🧠 Why I Built It

I’ve always believed the best design tools should disappear into the act of making.

AI tools are powerful, but most of them still require us to *think like machines* — to stop, describe, structure, and formalize what’s in our head before anything happens.

Drawbridge was my attempt to flip that dynamic.What if the act of *pointing and speaking* — the way we give feedback to each other — could become the way we collaborate with AI?

It’s not about replacing the conversation. It’s about making it more natural.

---

### 🎥 When AI Labs Found It

![](/assets/writing/drawbridge-featured-on-ai-labs/01.jpg)

Last week, **[AI Labs reviewed Drawbridge](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1cB2iqz_vnM&t=180s)** on their YouTube channel. I didn’t expect much — maybe a few comments or a couple of stars.

Instead, the repo blew up: **336 stars and 20 forks** (and counting).

Not massive numbers by open-source standards, but enough to make me sit back and realize — people actually *get* this.

And that’s the best feeling in the world.

---

### 💬 The Surreal Part

Here’s the twist: I’m not a developer. I’m a **designer** who just happens to love tinkering with code when curiosity strikes.

So seeing other developers fork Drawbridge, contribute ideas, and share how they’re using it… feels surreal. Like a tiny experiment suddenly stepped out into the world and started building bridges on its own.

---

### 🧩 The Bigger Picture

I think tools like Drawbridge hint at something deeper: that the interface between humans and AI is still wide open for invention.

We don’t just need smarter models — we need **smarter conversations** with them. Ones that feel visual, embodied, and alive.

Drawbridge is one small step toward that.
