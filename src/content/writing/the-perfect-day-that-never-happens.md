What a ridiculous thing to ask, right? Stay with me.

Of course, whenever I ask a friend or coworker their reflex response is—sitting on a beach with a cool drink in hand. Duh.

But no, try again.

What is real life for you? What are your real joys? What are your actual dreams? Your goals, your ambitions? What do you enjoy beyond lounging? And more importantly—what are your responsibilities? What are the things you *have* to do?

Now, imagine you’re in total control of every hour of your day. No meetings you didn’t sign up for. No unexpected obligations. Just you, your priorities, and your ideal timeline. What would that day look like?

### My (Almost) Perfect Morning

![](/assets/writing/the-perfect-day-that-never-happens/01.jpg)

I can hear you already— *Oh no, is this one of those hustle-porn posts?*

I promise, it’s not.

The point of defining a “Perfect Day” isn’t about executing it flawlessly.

The point is having a framework — a sense of how you *operate* when things go right.

And, maybe more importantly, understanding what to do when things don’t go the way you planned.

---

### Why Bother Mapping Out a Perfect Day?

I use this exercise for a few reasons:

To orient myself toward the things I *actually* want to accomplish.

To structure my time in a way that makes those things possible.

To have a *fallback plan* for when my day inevitably goes off the rails.

Because let’s be honest—I *almost never* have a perfect day.

Life happens. Priorities shift. Emergencies pop up. But when they do, I have my Perfect Day as an anchor. Instead of spiraling, I know what to return to. I know what “normal” looks like. And knowing what to do next? That’s half the battle.

---

### The Perfect Day Is Really About *You*

The deeper reason I do this? It’s not about productivity. It’s not about discipline. It’s about *having a relationship with yourself*.

Think about it: You spend your whole life with *you*. Every single day, you wake up and exist inside your own mind. And yet, so many of us never stop to ask—*What do I actually want?*

Not just in some vague, aspirational, five-years-from-now sense. But in the *actual, tangible sense of today*.

A “Perfect Day” is less about perfection and more about *awareness*. It forces you to confront your own desires, habits, and limitations. It makes you consider:

*What gives me energy, and what drains it?*

*What do I care about enough to protect time for?*

*When do I feel like my best self?*

And the real kicker? It shows you what *you're willing to compromise on* and what you’re not.

When you know what your Perfect Day looks like, you start to see when you’re too far from it. When things feel off. When you’re saying yes to the wrong things or neglecting what matters to you.

Most of us go through life reacting—meeting obligations, doing what’s expected, getting through the day. But when you design your Perfect Day, even if it never fully happens, you’re doing something different:

You’re making *intentional choices*.

You’re choosing to *know yourself*.

And that, more than anything, is the real value in all of this.

So, what does your Perfect Day look like? And how often does it happen?

### **A Simple Exercise to Define Your Perfect Day**

If you’ve never thought about your Perfect Day before, here’s a quick way to start.

**Grab a blank page (or your notes app).**

At the top, write: *"If I had full control of my time, what would my day look like?"*

**Break it down into three sections:Morning:** What time do you wake up? What’s the first thing you do? What sets the tone for your day?

**Afternoon:** What are you working on? Where are you? How do you want to feel?

**Evening:** How do you unwind? What rituals or habits help you close the day on a good note?

**Keep it real.**

Sure, you might want to wake up in a beachside villa in Bali, but what’s a version of this day that fits your *actual* life right now?

**Compare it to your real life.**

Where are the biggest gaps? What’s stopping your day from looking like this?

**Take one small step.**

What’s *one thing* from your Perfect Day that you can integrate into *tomorrow*? Maybe it’s a slow morning coffee instead of doomscrolling. Maybe it’s setting aside 30 minutes for something that actually excites you. Small steps compound over time.

The goal isn’t to create a rigid schedule or chase perfection. It’s simply to get to know yourself better—and maybe, just maybe, nudge your real life a little closer to the life you *want*.

So, go ahead—try it. And if you do, I’d love to hear what your Perfect Day looks like.
