I struck a nerve on LinkedIn last week, when I made a post titled “[Figma is dead](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tbreschi_design-webdev-cusror-activity-7402747374076678144-Eoj5?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_desktop&rcm=ACoAAADj7OMB2GF4qVORlY1bRDn5RovJODZg7qY).”

Conversations ensued about whether Figma still matters, whether designers should code, and whether abandoning a tool that’s created so much value makes sense. Worse, people got anxious, defensive, dismissive.

I wanted a real response about what’s happening in our industry - but after reflecting, I realized I prompted the wrong conversation.

This was never about “Figma”**

Figma is an incredible tool—arguably the best collaborative design environment ever built. I still use it—it’s genuinely great at what it does.

The tool isn’t the problem. The problem is what happens when the environment shifts and we don’t.

---

## Things are changing

The design landscape of 2025 doesn’t look like 2020. AI prototyping and agent-assisted iteration aren’t fringe experiments anymore. They’re becoming the terrain.

And when the terrain changes, the people that survive aren’t the strongest or the smartest. They’re the ones that adapt.

The anxiety I saw in that thread wasn’t really about Figma. It was about identity - about the discomfort of feeling the ground move beneath your feet.

---

## Evolution, not elimination

*Design isn’t going away, it’s changing.*

Adaptation isn’t about abandoning what you know. It’s about expanding what you’re capable of.

If a new tool or workflow helps you do better work, that’s not a threat — that’s evolution.

---

## Design is how it works

I want to get as close to the thing I’m building as possible. AI has made this possible. I can build UI that aligns to a styleguide, create interactions that animate the way I want, and understand how they work in our applications.

Approached intentionally, working with AI has the potential to **increase the fidelity of your work** and **deepen your understanding of what you’re contributing to**.

When you start building the real thing, you begin optimizing for outcomes. You optimize for customers, stakeholders, coworkers.

Not tools.

That’s not a betrayal of design. That’s design maturing.

---

## No shade

The point isn’t that Figma is irrelevant — it’s not - but treating any single tool as your identity leaves you brittle when things shift.

Not everyone needs to abandon Figma and start building in Cursor - but there is an opportunity to increase your skillset.

---

## So what now?

If you felt defensive reading that original post—or this one—that’s worth noticing. The discomfort is telling you something.

Figma isn’t going anywhere. But the environment around it is changing fast. The designers who thrive through this shift won’t be the ones who picked the “right” tool. They’ll be the ones who stayed adaptable.
